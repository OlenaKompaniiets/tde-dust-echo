What do time and temperature mean?

Local time is measured in the source rest frame relative to the unsmoothed fallback peak. At each point the stored solution is sampled at u = tlocal − r/c. These are simultaneous local snapshots, not images at one observer-arrival time.

The default temperature is ⟨T⟩N = ΣNaTa/ΣNa. The size selector shows the actual stored temperature for either end of the grain-size grid. Points outside the stored time support have no assigned temperature. Cold material is rendered more transparently; display opacity does not encode optical depth.

Background heating is absent in the model. Near-zero computed values before illumination do not measure the galaxy's quiescent dust temperature. Points sample an effective medium; their density is not a map of physical grain density.

Data and browser sampling

The browser uses every second original spatial sample (1152 points), and 101 local-time frames from 0 to 5000 d. Values are interpolated from thermal_solution.npz and rounded to 0.01 K for display. Full-precision scientific arrays remain in the repository. The camera orientation is for exploration and does not alter the fitted viewing inclination.

01 / THE IDEA

The infrared flare is a physical response.

The model starts with a hydrodynamical fallback history. It computes absorption, grain temperatures, thermal re-emission and light-travel delays. W1 and W2 are then predicted through the WISE bandpasses. No fixed dust-zone temperature or prescribed inter-band lag is fitted.

Accretion power illuminates the dust.

The evolving bolometric luminosity multiplies a fixed 30,000 K blackbody spectrum. Dust absorption is wavelength dependent. The calculation does not impose an Eddington cap or rescale to a chosen total event energy.

Escaping radiation is redshifted, converted to flux, integrated through the equal-energy W1/W2 response curves and calibrated against Vega. Fixed host fluxes are added before comparison with the observations.

MODEL SCOPE

Know what the calculation includes.

Computed: time-, radius- and grain-size-dependent temperatures, secondary heating, geometric delays, observer attenuation and absolute synthetic photometry.

Assumptions: an axisymmetric effective medium, graphite grains, a fixed source spectral shape and rapid thermal equilibrium. Scattering and steady background heating are omitted.

Static opacity: the solver checks temperatures against a sublimation threshold. It does not evolve irreversible grain destruction. Rendered points sample the effective medium.
