From fallback to infrared emission

Fallback and accretion

STARS provides tabulated hydrodynamical fallback histories. For fixed stellar and encounter parameters, the code applies the adopted black-hole mass scaling s = (MBH/106 M⊙)1/2: time scales by s and fallback rate by 1/s, preserving the returned mass. Source time zero is shifted to the unsmoothed fallback peak. Table interpolation is linear, with zero contribution outside the supplied support.

Ṁacc(t) = ∫−∞t Ṁfb(t′) K(t−t′) dt′
K(Δt) = exp(−Δt/τvisc) H(Δt) / τvisc

The zero-timescale case uses unsmoothed fallback. A pure circularisation time shift is fixed to zero. Luminosity is Lbol = ηṀaccc², with no imposed energy normalization or Eddington cap. The adopted isotropic illuminating spectrum is a 30,000 K blackbody, normalized on the transport wavelength grid.

Dust density and grains

Each radial population has n(r) ∝ r−p between its inner and outer radii. The angular domain is |cos θ| ≤ cos θopen. Opening angle and viewing inclination are measured from the symmetry axis. The radial gap and polar cavities are empty. Radial density values are volume averages within cells; normalization is imposed using radial absorption optical depths.

Case A uses graphite, a = 0.1–1 μm and dn/da ∝ a−3.5, discretized at 41 sizes. Absorption cross-sections are Cabs(λ,a) = πa²Qabs(λ,a), from the adopted Draine tables. The same cross-sections determine absorption and emission. Scattering is omitted.

τj = ⟨Cabs⟩source,size ∫ nj(r) dr

τC and τE are dimensionless optical depths. They are not time lags or independent band amplitudes.

Grain temperatures

∫ CabsJλ dλ = ∫ CabsBλ(T) dλ
Pλ = 4π CabsBλ(T)

The common factor 4π cancels in the equilibrium equation. Jλ includes attenuated direct light and absorbed thermal radiation from other cells. Temperature is solved separately for every cell, time, and grain size. Thermal equilibration is assumed rapid compared with resolved variability. The model does not include grain heat storage, stochastic heating, or steady host-galaxy heating.

Static-opacity domain

The graphite gate uses Tsub = 81200/[66.003 − ln(nH)] K, with the numerical nH in cm−3. At the adopted 109 cm−3, this is 1793.30 K. This density specifies a threshold assumption, not the fitted grain-density normalization. The gate is not grain-size dependent in this implementation. A failed gate means unsupported physics for this solver, not absence of a physical MIR response.

Follow the calculation

Expand each step for its physical meaning and implementation. The interactive explorer displays the stored solution; changing its controls does not refit these parameters.

1. Normalize the illuminating spectrum and dust column

The spectral shape sλ is Bλ(30,000 K) divided by its wavelength integral, so Lλ(t) = Lbol(t)sλ. Let w(a) be the normalized grain-number distribution. The source-weighted cross-section is ∫sλ∫w(a)Cabs(λ,a) da dλ. Dividing each specified radial optical depth by this cross-section fixes the corresponding radial grain column.

The optical depths therefore control how much source power is absorbed and how thermal radiation propagates. They are not arbitrary multiplicative normalizations of W1 or W2. The two populations share one driver, grain prescription and transport calculation.

2. Propagate radiation and solve the temperature

A ray crossing a column with absorption optical depth Δτ transmits a fraction exp(−Δτ) and deposits a fraction 1−exp(−Δτ). Ray paths through the effective axisymmetric medium supply cell-to-cell absorption probabilities and travel times. The code iterates thermal emission and secondary absorption until the emission field converges.

Absorbed power is partitioned among grain sizes using their number weights and absorption cross-sections. Each size has its own equilibrium temperature. The emitted luminosity of a cell is the sum of 4πCabsBλ(T) over its grain population. This maintains the absolute normalization set by the dust column and cell volume. Temperature differences and W1/W2 colour evolution arise from the radiation field and grain properties.

Implementation: src/v48_angular_rt.py, especially build_transport, direct_kernels and solve_time_rt. The model resolves an effective medium; a sampled point in a plot is not a separately fitted clump.

3. Keep source, local and observer times separate
u = tlocal − r/c
tobs = t₀ + (1+z)[tlocal − n̂·r/c]

The stored thermal solution uses the retarded coordinate u. A snapshot at one local time therefore samples different u values at different radii. For direct reprocessing, the observed delay relative to a photon emitted at the centre is (1+z)(r−n̂·r)/c. Secondary absorption adds the actual intervening path lengths. In the retarded coordinates of cells 1 and 2, the extra delay is (|r₂−r₁|+r₁−r₂)/c.

The event epoch t₀ maps the fallback-peak origin to MJD. It is not the disruption date. The 3D animation shows simultaneous local rest-frame slices; an observer image would require a different equal-arrival-time slice. Its 0–5000 d slider does not change the integration window of the solver.

4. Predict the calibrated WISE measurement

The observer transfer includes direction-dependent absorption and travel times. Redshift maps emitted wavelength to λobs = (1+z)λem. If Lλiso denotes the equivalent isotropic luminosity in the viewing direction, Fλ,obs = Lλ,emiso/[4πDL²(1+z)]. The adopted distance is 82.34 Mpc and z = 0.01895.

Fν,bquoted = Fν,0,b [∫ Rb(λ) Fλ(λ) dλ] / [∫ Rb(λ) Fλ,Vega(λ) dλ]

The official equal-energy response curves and the supplied Vega spectrum define the calibration. The zero-point flux densities are 309.540 Jy (W1) and 171.787 Jy (W2). An extra photon-counting λ/(hc) factor must not be applied to these equal-energy responses. Consistent wavelength units are required in both integrals. The attenuated source contribution and fixed host fluxes are included in the predicted total flux. See V48_LOCAL_PAIR/solver.py and the calibration sources under References and data.

5. Check energy and the domain of validity

Compare escaping thermal energy with directly absorbed source energy. Summing all intrinsic thermal emission counts energy again whenever an emitted photon is absorbed and re-emitted; that sum can exceed the directly absorbed energy without violating conservation.

EIR,escape ≈ Edirect,absorbed
Esource ≈ Edirect,escape + EIR,escape

These equalities require accounting for energy still travelling or awaiting re-emission at the end of a finite window. The archived Case A has negligible pending terms and a relative global budget residual about −1.09×10−6. Its maximum temperature is 1143.58 K, below the adopted 1793.30 K gate. Passing this gate supports using static opacity for this prescription. It does not establish that all possible grain populations would survive. Time-dependent grain destruction and opacity feedback are not implemented in this solver.

6. Compare to observations and interpret the result

The objective is the sum of photometric χ² contributions across 22 epochs and two bands. No additional lag, width, colour or fluence penalties are included. The supplied errors already include the adopted 1.10 mJy floor in quadrature. Applying that floor again would change the likelihood.

Case A fits six continuous physical parameters and the event epoch, with the STARS history and geometry radii held fixed and host fluxes fixed. Thus the reported nominal degrees of freedom are 44−7 = 37. Archived χ² = 58.95064. Searching a discrete family and possible parameter boundaries complicate formal goodness-of-fit interpretation; this nominal count is not a correction for the whole model-selection process.

Two photometric bands can constrain the observed response but do not uniquely reconstruct a three-dimensional dust distribution. Parameter intervals are conditional on the selected driver, grain prescription, geometry family and error model. Numerical convergence and profile convergence should be checked separately.
