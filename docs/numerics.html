Time coordinates, transport, and energy

Three time coordinates

The stored thermal coordinate is u = tlocal − r/c. Local time describes the physical state at the dust location. Observer time also includes the projection of that position toward the observer:

tobs = t₀ + (1+z)[tlocal − n̂·r/c]
Δtdirect,obs = (1+z)(r − n̂·r)/c

For a secondary path from r₁ to r₂, the delay in u is (|r₂−r₁| + r₁ − r₂)/c ≥ 0. Times must be converted to the same units. t₀ maps the fallback peak to MJD; it is not a measured disruption date.

Computational sequence

  1. Load the physical configuration, photometry, STARS curve, grain data, and WISE calibration.
  2. Construct the smoothed luminosity history and conservatively integrate it over transport time bins.
  3. Build the spherical-radial and polar thermal cells and normalize grain numbers using τC and τE.
  4. Compute direct illumination and thermal transfer kernels using three-dimensional paths through the geometry.
  5. Iterate absorbed power and thermal emission, including delayed secondary heating.
  6. Check the temperature gate and energy ledger; propagate emission toward the observer.
  7. Integrate W1/W2 signals and profile the event epoch against the photometry.

Convergence checks

The iteration monitors maximum spectral change and an L1 change in absorbed power; the implementation uses a 10−6 tolerance with an iteration limit. This measures iterative convergence at fixed discretization. Resolution sensitivity must be checked independently by changing temporal, angular, radial, and spectral resolution.

Energy accounting

Compare total eventually escaping IR energy with directly absorbed source energy, accounting for pending propagation. Intrinsic thermal emission counts photons each time they are re-emitted and can exceed the directly absorbed energy because of recycling. It must not be interpreted as net escaping IR energy.

The ledger also distinguishes full-driver energy from the source energy within the simulated window. A small global energy residual is a conservation check, not a guarantee of a converged light curve or a unique inferred geometry.
